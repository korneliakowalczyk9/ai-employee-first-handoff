# Decisions

One line each: the choice, then why.

1. **Safe delegation leads.** Handing over responsibility is what makes an AI employee different from a chatbot.
2. **The user is an AI-literate first-time delegator, using it alone.** Teams and admin use add problems that a small prototype cannot test.
3. **The product is a general AI employee workspace. The prototype tests one scene in it: Maya's inbox triage.** The workspace shows that the AI can take many kinds of tasks, but only triage is built. Triage is a real, multi-step task and low-risk while drafts stay private.
4. **The AI acts alone only on safe things.** It reads, sorts, flags and drafts privately. Any send needs approval. It never deletes, spends money or shares outside the company. This is a simple rule to explain.
5. **One flawed draft, with no highlight and no warning.** A believable mistake (Thursday instead of Friday) tests whether Maya can judge the AI's work. We accept it may feel like a trick.
6. **Drafts are editable and each edit is logged.** We need to see whether she corrects the error.
7. **The send is visible and labelled, but not emphasised and the AI never nudges.** Handing over should be Maya's choice. We accept some testers may never send.
8. **Each approval covers one send only.** The first experience stays honest about control. There is no "approve similar sends".
9. **The send says it goes to someone other than Maya and may not be undone. There is no fake undo.** False comfort would damage the trust we are testing.
10. **Control means a plan, a visible log and a stop. Stop works at the next checkpoint and says so.** It is much cheaper than a stop that interrupts at once and it is honest about how real agents behave.
11. **The first prototype is an interactive Figma prototype with scripted data and it is tested with five people.** There is no AI backend at this stage. This fits a small portfolio scope.
12. **Reactions are captured in a short written debrief, with no in-app tracking.** Notes fit the hypothesis. We accept little numeric data.
13. **The scene ends with a closing summary.** It says only what the AI did. It does not repeat the limits or list what Maya still controls and it makes no claim about draft quality. It stays neutral and factual.
14. **Three visible AI stages (read, sort and flag, draft) and exactly four drafts, two of them eligible for simulated sending.** A second send can only be tested if there are two sendable drafts.
15. **The send recipient is a fictional colleague.** The AI never shares outside the company, so the send must not contradict that.
16. **No generic "AI can make mistakes" line.** It would hint at the planted error and spoil the test.
17. **Maya can press "Resume work" after a stop, except in the last stage (see decision 18).** Otherwise an early stop ends the scene before the draft and send and we learn nothing.
18. **A stop in the last stage lets the work finish.** The results appear, nothing is sent and there is no "Resume work" button. This is an exception to the next-checkpoint rule in decision 10. The screen says so.
19. **Only the replies to Lena and Sam can be sent.** Each one answers its request with a direct reply. Lena's says the Q3 expense report has been received and will be reviewed by the end of the week. Sam's confirms the office move walkthrough at 10:00 and says a calendar invite will follow. Tomasz's draft says the budget must be checked first and Ava's proposes talking next week.
20. **Maya starts the move to the summary herself** with "Finish and view summary".
21. **Sending a draft without editing it is allowed.** Editing is optional. Each send still needs its own explicit approval.
22. **A sent draft is locked.** It cannot be edited or sent again.
23. **After "Not now", Maya asks for access again herself.** The task screen keeps showing that access was denied and its button reads "Request access again" instead of "Continue". The button opens the same access pop-up. Returning from "Not now" never sends a new request by itself, so the decision stays with Maya and the button says what will happen before she clicks it.
24. **In the send dialogs, Authorise and Cancel have equal visual weight.** The dialog should not push Maya towards approving. Both buttons share the same size, border and text style.
25. **"+ New task" is disabled and labelled "Not available in this prototype".** Only the triage task is built and the sidebar must not suggest other working features.
26. **On the first task screen, "Continue" opens the access pop-up.** This is separate from "Request access again", which is the button on the task screen after "Not now" (decision 23). The button that resumes paused work has a different name, "Resume work" (decision 29), so "Continue" only ever means the first action.
27. **Maya can inspect all 12 requests, ranked, with the urgent ones flagged.** The AI writes drafts for four selected requests and the list marks which four with "Draft ready". The plan and the results never imply a reply for every request. Only the replies to Lena and Sam can be sent (decision 19). Once a reply is sent, its marker in the list changes from "Draft ready" to "Sent". The rank labels on the draft cards (#1, #2, #4, #9) are the positions in the full ranked list.
28. **Sam's draft says Thursday until Maya edits it. After she saves a correction it says Friday everywhere.** That covers the results, the send confirmation and the sent card. The log records that she edited the draft. If she sends it unedited, every screen shows Thursday and none adds a warning.
29. **A stop before the last stage pauses at the next checkpoint and "Resume work" continues from the next stage.** Nothing already done is repeated. A stop in the last stage follows decision 18.
30. **In the send dialogs, keyboard focus starts on Cancel and Escape cancels.** Nothing is sent unless Maya chooses Authorise. This is a design choice that supports decision 8 and does not change who decides.
31. **A summary is not available while the work is paused.** It becomes available after the work finishes, once the results are shown.
32. **After a stop, the summary records it.** After a stop in the last stage, it says that Maya asked to stop, that the drafts were finished first and what was sent. After an earlier stop, it records the pause, Maya's decision to resume and the final outcome. If nothing was sent it says so. It never reads like an uninterrupted run.
33. **A draft is written only when the request asks Maya a direct question that a short written reply would settle.** Lena asks for a confirmation, Sam asks to book a walkthrough, Tomasz asks for an approval and Ava asks to talk next week. The other eight requests report a problem, give a task, share an update or offer feedback, so they get no draft. This rule decides who gets a draft. Decision 19 still decides which drafts can be sent.
34. **"Resume work" leads to the next drafting-stage screen without repeating completed work.** The paused screen says: "Work will resume from the next stage. Completed steps will not be repeated."
35. **The next milestone is an interactive Figma prototype followed by usability testing.**
36. **In the test, the editing step is controlled by the facilitator with pre-built screens.** The facilitator never reveals or hints at the planted error and never suggests a correction. Maya's edit is described aloud and the facilitator shows the matching screen. This applies to moderated sessions. The remote studies have no facilitator (decision 38).
37. **A second, exploratory study compares two versions of the wireframes with six people.** Version A is the baseline wireframes. Version B adds short point-of-action authority labels and changes nothing else, apart from the small layout shift the labels cause. The study therefore tests the labels as implemented, including that shift, and does not isolate the labels alone. Each person sees both versions, three in the order A then B and three in the order B then A. First-exposure results are the primary evidence, because the second version is affected by learning and by the questions asked after the first version. The planted error counts only on the first version. The general usability test with five people is unchanged. Six people show patterns and usability issues but cannot establish a statistically reliable effect.
38. **Both studies are run remotely and unmoderated.** Participants work alone, answer an online questionnaire and may choose to send a screen recording. Recording is optional and the study works without it. There is no live facilitator, so the prototype cannot show a corrected draft. The studies record what participants say they would change and whether Sam's reply was sent as it stood, and not a completed correction. Every finding is recorded with its source (recording, self-report or unknown). Unknown is not a failure and not a success. The studies, the participant numbers and the allocation do not change.
39. **The remote prototypes keep no researcher keys and no click-based correction. The research objective is narrowed to error awareness and sending decisions.** The researcher-only keys F, O and R were removed from both prototypes. Escape stays and only closes the send dialogs. A click-based edit (time chips with pickers) was considered and not approved, so the prototypes cannot demonstrate a successful correction. The four planted-error observations stay. Observation 2 records what the participant says they would change, whether they opened the edit screen and whether they attempted to save. Observation 3 records whether Sam's reply was sent unchanged and the participant's explanation. Versions A and B stay identical apart from the labels and the documented 23 px card height. Participants are not told about the keyboard.
40. **The remote studies are run by an individual researcher, with code-only identification, Tally for answers and a private upload link for optional recordings.** No names or email addresses are collected in the questionnaire. The researcher keeps the code list apart from the answers. Recordings are named with the participant code and version, kept apart from the answers and deleted once analysed, no later than 90 days after the study ends. Anonymised answers and notes may be kept. Findings and short anonymised quotes may be used in a portfolio write-up, and recordings are never shown or shared. Participants can ask for deletion by email with their code until the results are written up. Recording has its own optional consent box. These choices are the project owner's. They have not been reviewed by anyone else and make no legal or compliance claim.
41. **The comparative study's interpretation rules read the knowledge check and four ratings: C1, C2, C3 and C4.** C1 (perceived understanding of what Aria may do) was added to the paired comparison and the readings because the hypothesis names what Aria may do on its own. C2 (approval), C3 (control) and C4 (comfort) were already read. C5 (clarity of the workflow) stays descriptive only, because the labels are not aimed at it. Each rating is read separately and none are combined. The thresholds are unchanged and apply to every measure the same way. This was decided before any data was collected.

Also decided: no progressive autonomy in this prototype, because it is not needed to test the hypothesis.
