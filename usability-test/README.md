# Usability testing

There are two separate studies. They use the same Figma prototype and the same fictional scenario, but they ask different questions.

| | General usability test | Comparative study |
|---|---|---|
| Question | Can people hand a first task to an AI employee and feel in control? | Do short authority labels at the point of action change what people understand about what Aria may do and what needs approval? |
| Participants | Five | Six |
| What they see | One version, the baseline wireframes and prototype | Both versions, one after the other: Version A (the baseline screens) and Version B (the same screens with labels) |
| Order | Not applicable | Three people see A then B and three see B then A |
| Primary evidence | The whole session | First-exposure observations and responses, meaning the first version each person sees. Second-exposure results are supplementary because of learning and questionnaire priming |
| Session procedure | [test-plan.md](test-plan.md) | The same [test-plan.md](test-plan.md), with the differences listed in [comparative-study/README.md](comparative-study/README.md) |
| Materials | [test-plan.md](test-plan.md), [facilitator-guide.md](facilitator-guide.md), [observation-template.md](observation-template.md) | [comparative-study/](comparative-study/README.md) |

The general [test-plan.md](test-plan.md) is the source of the standard session procedure for both studies. The comparative study adds its own facilitator instructions, participant response template, observation sheet and analysis template, and these govern version order, participant responses, observations and analysis for that study. The general study's design of five participants and one version does not govern the comparative study.

Both are exploratory. Five or six people can show patterns and usability problems. They cannot establish a statistically reliable effect. Keep the findings of the two studies separate.
