# Usability testing

Two separate studies use the same Figma prototype and the same fictional scenario but ask different questions. Both are **remote and unmoderated**: participants work alone, answer an online questionnaire and may choose to send a screen recording. Both are exploratory. They can show patterns and usability problems, not statistically reliable effects. Keep their findings separate.

| | General usability test | Comparative study |
|---|---|---|
| Question | Can people hand a first task to an AI employee and feel in control? | Do short authority labels at the point of action change what people understand about what Aria may do and what needs approval? |
| Participants | Five | Six |
| What they see | Version A, the baseline, only | Both versions, one after the other (A then B for P1, P3, P5 and B then A for P2, P4, P6) |
| Primary evidence | The whole submission | First-exposure evidence. Second-exposure evidence is supplementary |
| Participant instructions | [general](participant-materials/general-study-instructions.md) | [comparative](participant-materials/comparative-study-instructions.md) |

The prototype cannot demonstrate a completed correction, so both studies focus on error awareness and sending decisions (decision 39). It has no real AI and no interaction analytics.
