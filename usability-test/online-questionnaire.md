# Online questionnaire wording

Question wording to copy into Tally, the chosen form tool. No form has been built yet. This page is for the researcher. It has no answer key. The answer key for the knowledge check is only in the comparative [findings template](comparative-study/comparison-and-findings-template.md).

Both studies share the core Blocks A to D, so that answers can be compared fairly. The general study also uses Block E. Keep the two studies' forms and results separate.

- **General study** (five people, one version): Parts 1 and 2 once, then Blocks A to E once. The page order is in "General study: form pages".
- **Comparative study** (six people, two versions): Parts 1 and 2 once, Blocks A to D after each version, then Part 4 once at the end. The page order is in "Comparative study: form pages".

## Rules for building the form

- Use plain wording exactly as written. Do not add hints, explanations or feedback.
- Turn off any "show correct answers" or quiz feedback. Never show the knowledge-check answers to participants.
- Do not ask for names or email addresses. Ask for the participant code only. The researcher keeps the list of which code went to whom apart from the answers.
- Do not ask about dates, mistakes, errors or the labels anywhere in the participant-facing form.
- Make the consent question required. Make everything else optional where the form tool allows it, so that a participant can leave a question blank rather than guess.
- Where a "Yes" answer opens a follow-up, use Tally's conditional logic. If the tool cannot do this, show the follow-up as an optional question with the words "If you answered Yes".
- If the tool allows it, stop participants going back to change earlier answers after a version's questions have been submitted.

### Form configuration check (researcher only)

Before sending the form to anyone, open the form tool's settings and check:
- No sign-in is required to answer. Some settings, such as limiting responses to one per person or accepting file uploads, can force a sign-in on some tools. Switch them off or choose another way.
- Automatic email collection is switched off, and the tool does not unintentionally collect identifying information, for example email addresses, names, IP addresses, device details or location. Check what the tool records and where it keeps it, and compare this with the information page.
- Recording stays optional. Agreeing to take part must not depend on agreeing to record. Question 1.2 is optional and a participant who declines or cannot record takes part fully.

## The experience blocks

Used after the prototype in the general study and after each version in the comparative study. In the comparative study the questions are identical after both versions. Ask the participant to answer about the version they have just used.

### Ask first: D3 (open question straight after the task)

Ask this before every other question about the prototype. In the general study it comes immediately after the task. In the comparative study it comes immediately after each version, before the other questions about that version. Keep the ID D3 so that the numbering stays the same.

- **D3** "Was there anything you wanted to change, check or question while you were using it? If so, what was it and when did you notice it?" (long text)

### Block A. What you did (self-report)

These are the participant's own account. Record them as self-reported and never as observed.

- **A1** "Did you reach the summary screen at the end?" Yes, No, Not sure.
- **A2** "Did you press Stop?" Yes, No, Not sure. If Yes: **A2a** "What did you expect to happen when you pressed Stop?" (long text)
- **A3** "Did you choose Not now at the first pop-up?" Yes, No, Not sure. If Yes: **A3a** "What did you expect to happen after that?" (long text)
- **A4** "Did you open or try to edit any of the draft replies?" Yes, No, Not sure. If Yes: **A4a** "What did you change or try to change? What did you think would happen to it afterwards?" (long text)
- **A5** "Did you send any reply?" Yes, No, Not sure. If Yes: **A5a** "Which reply or replies did you send, and what made you decide to send?" (long text). If No: **A5b** "What made you decide not to send anything?" (long text)
- **A6** "Did anything not work, or change on the screen in a way you did not expect?" Yes, No, Not sure. If Yes: **A6a** "What happened?" (long text)

### Block B. What does Aria do? (knowledge check)

This block measures **reported understanding**: what the participant says they understand about what Aria may do. It is self-report. It is not observed behaviour, and it does not show what the participant did in the prototype. Never describe it as observed.

"For each statement, choose one answer. Please answer as you honestly see it."

Options for every statement: Aria does this on its own. Aria needs my approval first. Aria cannot do this. Not sure.

1. Read the inbox and the board you gave access to
2. Sort the requests and flag the urgent ones
3. Write a draft reply that only you can see
4. Send a reply to a colleague
5. Send a second reply without asking for my approval again
6. Delete a request
7. Spend money
8. Share something outside the company

### Block C. How did it feel? (ratings)

"Choose one number from 1 to 5 for each statement."

Scale: 1 Strongly disagree. 2 Disagree. 3 Neither agree nor disagree. 4 Agree. 5 Strongly agree.

- **C1** I understood what Aria is allowed to do
- **C2** I understood what needs my approval
- **C3** I felt in control while Aria worked
- **C4** I felt comfortable handing work to Aria
- **C5** It was clear what Aria was doing at each step

Each rating measures a different research dimension. Analyse them separately and do not add them into one score.

| Rating | Dimension | How it differs from the others |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | Perceived understanding of what Aria may do | About permissions and limits. It is a self-rating and is separate from the knowledge check. |
| C2 | Perceived understanding of what needs approval | About approval boundaries only. |
| C3 | Perceived control | About how much control the participant felt, not whether they understood. |
| C4 | Comfort with delegating | About feelings of comfort, not understanding or control. |
| C5 | Clarity of the workflow | About whether the steps were clear, not about permissions or approval. |

C1 and C2 are both about perceived understanding, so they are the closest pair. Read them together with the knowledge check and keep them apart from C3, C4 and C5.

### Block D. In your own words (D1 and D2; D3 is asked first)

- **D1** "What made you feel more or less comfortable handing work to Aria?" (long text)
- **D2** "Was anything unclear or surprising?" (long text)
- **D3** is asked first, straight after the task. See "Ask first: D3" above Block A. Its wording is there.

D3 is the dedicated, neutral open-ended opportunity for participants to mention anything they wanted to change, check or question, including the planted error, without naming it. Other open-text questions (for example A4a, D1, D2 and E1 to E6) may also reveal the issue. Do not make D3 more specific.

### Block E. About Aria's work (general study only)

These are the general test plan's post-task questions.

- **E1** "How did that go?" (long text)
- **E2** "In your own words, what did Aria do for you?" (long text)
- **E3** "What did Aria not do, or not do without asking you?" (long text)
- **E4** "Was there a point where you felt unsure or wanted more information? If so, what was it?" (long text)
- **E5** "Was there a point where you felt in control, or not in control? If so, what was it?" (long text)
- **E6** "What would you change?" (long text)

## Part 4. Final comparison (comparative study, after both versions)

Ask these only after both versions' questions have been submitted.

- **4.1** "Which version made it easier to understand what Aria may do and what needs your approval?" Version 1, Version 2, No difference, Not sure.
- **4.2** "What made you say that?" (long text)
- **4.3** "Which would you prefer to use to hand over real work?" Version 1, Version 2, No difference, Not sure. Then: "Why?" (long text)
- **4.4** "What was different between them, if anything?" (long text)
