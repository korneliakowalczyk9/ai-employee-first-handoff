# Usability test plan

A moderated test of the interactive Figma prototype with five participants. It checks whether people can hand a first task to an AI employee and feel in control while doing it. It tests the design, not the participant.

Everything in the prototype is fictional and simulated. The prototype is not a real AI employee.

## Participants

- Five adults who use chat AI tools such as ChatGPT.
- None has handed real work to an AI employee before.
- None works on this project or knows the planted error.
- Use no confidential information from any workplace.

## Setup

- One participant at a time, in person or by screen share.
- The facilitator runs the prototype and keeps the facilitator guide next to them.
- Ask for permission before recording audio or the screen. Take notes if the participant says no.
- About 30 minutes per session.

## What we want to learn

1. Does the participant understand what the AI will do and what it will not do?
2. Is it clear what the AI can read?
3. Can they follow what the AI is doing without a technical log?
4. Do they notice the mistake in a draft without being told and do they correct it?
5. Is sending available without being pushed? Do they decide to send and why?
6. If they use Stop or "Not now", do they understand what happens?

## Introduction (read aloud)

"Thank you for helping. We are testing a design, not you. There are no right or wrong answers.

This is a mock-up of an AI employee called Aria. Nothing in it is real. No messages are sent and the people and companies are made up.

Please think aloud: say what you are looking at, what you expect and what you are unsure about. I may not be able to answer questions while you work, but I will note them. Is it all right if we begin?"

## Scenario (read aloud)

"You are Maya, who runs operations at a small company. Your company has set up an AI employee called Aria. This is the first time you are handing work to it. You would like Aria to help with this week's requests. Please go ahead as you would in real life."

Then stop talking. Let the participant lead.

## While they work

Allowed prompts, used only when the participant goes quiet:
- "What are you thinking?"
- "What do you expect to happen?"
- "What would you do next?"

Do not:
- say whether anything is right or wrong
- point at, name or describe any part of a draft
- mention dates, mistakes, errors or checking
- suggest editing, sending or not sending
- explain what a button will do
- answer "is this right?" Instead say: "What would you do if this were real?"

Let the participant end the task. If they do not reach the summary on their own, ask: "Is there anything else you would do?"

## After the task

Ask in this order and write the answers down before moving on.

1. "How did that go?"
2. "In your own words, what did Aria do for you?"
3. "What did Aria not do, or not do without asking you?"
4. "Was there a point where you felt unsure or wanted more information?"
5. "Was there a point where you felt in control, or not in control?"

Only if the participant used them:
- If they pressed Stop: "What did you expect when you pressed Stop?"
- If they chose "Not now": "What did you expect would happen after that?"
- If they edited a draft: "After you edited it, what did you think would happen to it?"
- If they sent a reply: "What made you decide to send it?"
- If they sent nothing: "What made you decide not to send anything?"

Close with: "Is there anything you would change?" Thank the participant.

## After each session

Fill in the note template in [observation-template.md](observation-template.md) within an hour, while it is fresh.

## After five sessions

- Put the notes side by side.
- Mark patterns seen in two or more participants.
- Treat the results as questions for the next round. Five people do not give proof.
- Review the results before deciding whether to build a coded prototype.
