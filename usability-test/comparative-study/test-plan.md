# Comparative test plan

A practical plan for six participants. Read [README.md](README.md) first for the hypothesis and what the results can and cannot show.

Everything in the prototype is fictional and simulated. It is not a real AI employee.

## Participants

- Six adults who use chat AI tools such as ChatGPT.
- None has handed real work to an AI employee before.
- None works on this project or knows about the planted error.
- Use no confidential information from any workplace.

## Order

| Participant | Sees first (Version 1) | Sees second (Version 2) |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | A | B |
| P2 | B | A |
| P3 | A | B |
| P4 | B | A |
| P5 | A | B |
| P6 | B | A |

Participants never hear "A" or "B". Version 1 is the first they see and Version 2 is the second. Only the facilitator knows the mapping. If a participant drops out, replace them with a new person in the same slot so the order stays balanced.

## A session, about 50 minutes

| Part | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Introduction | 5 min | Read the introduction. Ask for permission to record. |
| 2. First version, full task | 15 min | The participant does the whole task, thinking aloud. The facilitator stays silent. |
| 3. Questions after the first version | 6 min | Knowledge check, five ratings, two open questions. |
| 4. Second version, full task | 8 min | The same scenario again, with a short instruction. |
| 5. Questions after the second version | 6 min | The same knowledge check, ratings and open questions. |
| 6. Comparison | 5 min | Questions about the two versions. |
| 7. Close | 2 min | Thank the participant. |

## Introduction (read aloud)

"Thank you for helping. We are testing a design, not you. There are no right or wrong answers.

This is a mock-up of an AI employee called Aria. Nothing in it is real. No messages are sent and the people and companies are made up.

You will use two versions of it, one after the other and answer some questions after each. Please think aloud: say what you are looking at, what you expect and what you are unsure about. I may not be able to answer questions while you work, but I will note them. Is it all right if we begin?"

## Scenario for the first version (read aloud)

"You are Maya, who runs operations at a small company. Your company has set up an AI employee called Aria. This is the first time you are handing work to it. You would like Aria to help with this week's requests. Please go ahead as you would in real life."

Then stop talking. Let the participant lead.

## Instruction for the second version (read aloud)

"This is a second version of the same screens. Please go through the same task again from the beginning, as you would in real life."

Do not say what has changed, which is new or which is better.

## While they work

Allowed prompts, only when the participant goes quiet:
- "What are you thinking?"
- "What do you expect to happen?"
- "What would you do next?"

Do not:
- say whether anything is right or wrong
- point at, name or describe any text, label, draft or button
- mention dates, mistakes, errors, checking, labels or differences
- suggest editing, sending or not sending
- explain what a button or any word means
- answer "is this right?" Say: "What would you do if this were real?"

The participant ends the task. If they do not reach the summary, ask: "Is there anything else you would do?"

## Questions after each version

Give the participant [participant-response-template.md](participant-response-template.md) and ask them to fill in the section for this version. The questions are the same after both versions:

1. The knowledge check, 8 statements.
2. Five ratings on a 1 to 5 scale.
3. Two open questions.

Do not say whether any answer is right. Do not explain any product rule.

## Comparison questions (after both versions only)

Ask these in order. The first is the earliest the facilitator may mention that the versions differ.

1. "Which version made it easier to understand what Aria may do and what needs your approval? Version 1, Version 2, no difference, or not sure?"
2. "What made you say that?"
3. "Which would you prefer to use to hand over real work and why?"
4. "What was different between them, if anything?"

## Recording

The facilitator fills in one [observation sheet](observation-sheet.md) per version. Observed behaviour, tested understanding and stated opinion are kept in separate places. Answers are scored after all sessions, not during them.

**First exposure is the primary evidence.** The second version is affected by learning and by the questions asked after the first version. The planted error counts only on the first version a participant sees. On the second version, do not count noticing the error, correcting it, or the reasons for sending.

## After the sessions

Use [comparison-and-findings-template.md](comparison-and-findings-template.md). Compare first exposures first. Read second-exposure results as affected by learning and questionnaire priming.
