## 10. Working rules for interpreting the results

These rules were set before any data was collected, so that the interpretation is not decided afterwards. They are not statistical tests. Even when "Tentatively supported" applies, it is not evidence of a reliable effect. With six people, a result like 5 of 6 can easily arise by chance.

| Reading | When it applies |
|---|---|
| Tentatively supported | The first-exposure groups differ in the same direction as the hypothesis and at least 5 of 6 paired differences point the same way and comments mention clarity or approval. |
| Mixed | The first-exposure groups and the paired differences disagree, or only 3 or 4 of 6 point the same way. |
| No clear difference | Scores differ by 1 point or less, or the direction is split. |
| Contrary | Version B is lower on the first exposure and in at least 5 of 6 paired differences. |

State which reading applies for each measure: knowledge check, R2 approval, R3 control, R4 comfort.

First-exposure results come first. The paired differences can support a first-exposure difference but can never establish one on their own, because they are affected by learning and questionnaire priming.
