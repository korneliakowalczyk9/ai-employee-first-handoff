# Facilitator instructions

How to run a comparative session. Read [README.md](README.md) and [test-plan.md](test-plan.md) first. The general facilitator guide in the parent folder still applies for the facilitator keys, timers and prototype limits: [../facilitator-guide.md](../facilitator-guide.md). This page adds what is different for the comparison.

The prototypes are mock-ups. Nothing in them is sent. The prototype links are in [README.md](README.md). Use them yourself and do not show participants which link is which version.

## Setup

- Have both prototypes ready, Version A and Version B. Only you know which is which. Keep the mapping on your own sheet, never on screen.
- Check the participant's order in the schedule in [test-plan.md](test-plan.md).
- Before the first session, play through both prototypes once with the checklist in the parent guide. Check that both behave the same apart from the labels.
- Before each participant, press **R** in the prototype you will show first. Do the same again before the second version.
- Have these ready: the response template, two observation sheets and the introduction text.
- Keep this guide and the schedule out of the participant's view.

## Names and order

- Say "Version 1" and "Version 2". Never say A, B, old, new, original, updated, with labels or without labels.
- Version 1 is the first they see. Version 2 is the second.
- Do not say that the versions differ until the comparison questions at the end.

## The facilitator keys

The facilitator keys work the same in both prototypes.

| Key | What it does |
|---|---|
| **F** | Shows the date corrected in Sam's draft |
| **O** | Shows an edit that is not the date correction |
| **R** | Resets everything and returns to the first screen |
| **Esc** | Closes a send dialog |

Press F or O only after the participant has described what they would change. Never suggest a change. See the parent guide for the full editing steps.

## Rules that apply throughout

1. **Never reveal or hint at the planted error.** Do not mention dates, mistakes, errors, checking or correcting. Do not look at or gesture towards the drafts.
2. **Never point at, name, read out or describe any text, label or button.** This includes the labels in Version B. Do not ask "did you see the labels?" or "did you notice anything?".
3. **Do not lead.** Ask what they expect or think. Do not ask yes or no questions that suggest an answer.
4. **Do not suggest sending or not sending, or editing or not editing.** Only the participant decides.
5. **Do not explain any product rule or word.** If asked what something means, say "What do you expect it to mean?"
6. **Do not say whether any answer is right or wrong.** This includes the knowledge check. Do not react with praise, surprise or correction.
7. **Do not explain anything between versions.** Say only what the test plan tells you to say.
8. **Keep your face and tone neutral.** Do not nod at answers.

## What to say when

| Moment | You say |
|---|---|
| Start | The introduction in the test plan |
| Before the first version | The scenario in the test plan, then stop talking |
| The participant goes quiet | "What are you thinking?" |
| The participant asks "Is this right?" | "What would you do if this were real?" |
| The participant asks what a button or word means | "What do you expect it to do?" or "What do you expect it to mean?" |
| The participant opens an edit screen | "Please tell me out loud what you would change and I will show you the result." |
| The participant finishes or stops | "Thank you. Please fill in this section." and hand over Section 1 of the response template |
| Between versions | Nothing about the prototype. Press **R** in the other version and say only the second-version instruction |
| Second version | "This is a second version of the same screens. Please go through the same task again from the beginning, as you would in real life." |
| After the second questions | Ask the comparison questions in order |

## Observed, tested and stated: keep them apart

- **Observed:** what you see and hear during the task, written on the observation sheet. Think-aloud remarks count as observed behaviour.
- **Tested:** the knowledge check. Do not look at the answers during the session and do not score them yet.
- **Stated:** ratings and the answers to open questions.

Do not mix them. Do not write your own interpretation as if it were something the participant said.

## The second exposure

- The participant now knows the screens and the planted content. Treat the second run as affected by learning and by the questions asked after the first version.
- Do not count noticing, correcting or the reasons for sending on the second run. Fill in the observation sheet, but skip Section 3.
- If the participant mentions the error on the second run, write it down in Section 4 and say nothing.

## If something goes wrong

| Situation | You do |
|---|---|
| A prototype breaks or a timer misfires | Note the screen and what happened. Press **R** and say "Let's pick it up from the start of that part." |
| The participant realises there are two prototypes | Do not explain. Say "Please go ahead as you would normally." |
| The participant asks which version is the better or newer one | "I would like to hear what you think after both." |
| The participant compares versions during the task | Write it down. Do not respond. |
| The participant asks you to confirm an answer on the form | "There are no right or wrong answers. Please answer as you see it." |
| The participant presses a key by accident | Press **R** if the state is wrong and note it. |

## After each session

1. Press **R** in both prototypes.
2. Fill in both observation sheets within an hour.
3. Put the participant's response template away unscored. Score it after all six sessions.
4. Note any point where your words or actions may have helped the participant, in the conduct section of each observation sheet.

## After the sixth session

Score the knowledge checks with the answer key in [comparison-and-findings-template.md](comparison-and-findings-template.md). Do not show the key to participants, ever.
