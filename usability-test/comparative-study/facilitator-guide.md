# Facilitator instructions

How to run a comparative session. Read [README.md](README.md) first for the study design, the order of exposure and the session timings. The standard session procedure is in the general [test plan](../test-plan.md) and applies to this study too: the introduction, the scenario, the neutral prompts and what the facilitator must not do. This page adds what is different for the comparison and is not a second test plan. The general facilitator guide in the parent folder still applies for the facilitator keys, timers and prototype limits: [../facilitator-guide.md](../facilitator-guide.md).

The prototypes are mock-ups. They do not send any real messages. The prototype links are in [README.md](README.md). Use them yourself and do not show participants which link is which version.

## Setup

- Have both prototypes ready, Version A and Version B. Only you know which is which. Keep the mapping on your own sheet, never on screen.
- Check the participant's order in the schedule in [README.md](README.md).
- Before the first session, play through both prototypes once with the checklist in the parent guide.
- The two prototypes are not identical apart from the labels. The documented differences are the authority labels in Version B and the 23 px extra height of the Version B results screens. Check that nothing else differs, and that neither prototype ever leads to a screen of the other.
- Before each participant, press **R** in the prototype you will show first. Do the same again before the second version.
- Have these ready: the response template, two observation sheets and the introduction text.
- Keep this guide and the schedule out of the participant's view.

## Names and order

- Say "Version 1" and "Version 2". Never say A, B, old, new, original, updated, baseline, with labels or without labels.
- Version 1 is the first they see. Version 2 is the second.
- Do not say that the versions differ until the comparison questions at the end.

## What differs from the standard session

Follow the general test plan, with these changes.

**Before the introduction.** Ask the standard familiarity question from the general test plan, "How do you use chat AI tools such as ChatGPT?" Write the answer on the first-exposure observation sheet, in the participant's words.

**Introduction.** Read the standard introduction, with one extra sentence after "No messages are sent and the people and companies are made up.":

"You will use two versions of it, one after the other, and answer some questions after each."

**Scenario.** Read the standard scenario for the first version, then stop talking.

**Second version.** After Section 1 is complete, press **R** in the other prototype and say only:

"This is a second version of the same screens. Please go through the same task again from the beginning, as you would in real life."

Do not say what has changed, which is new or which is better.

**Questions after each version.** Do not ask the general plan's post-task questions. Instead hand over the response template and ask the participant to fill in the section for the version they have just used: Section 1 after the first version and Section 2 after the second. Both sections have the same questions. Do not say whether any answer is right. Do not explain any product rule.

**Comparison, Section 3.** After both versions, ask these four questions aloud, in order, and write the answers down in the participant's own words. This is the earliest you may mention that the versions differ.

1. "Which version made it easier to understand what Aria may do and what needs your approval? Version 1, Version 2, no difference, or not sure?"
2. "What made you say that?"
3. "Which would you prefer to use to hand over real work and why?"
4. "What was different between them, if anything?"

## The facilitator keys

The facilitator keys work the same in both prototypes.

| Key | What it does |
|---|---|
| **F** | Shows the date corrected in Sam's draft |
| **O** | Shows an edit that is not the date correction |
| **R** | Resets everything and returns to the first screen |
| **Esc** | Closes a send dialog |

Press F or O only after the participant has described what they would change. Never suggest a change. See the parent guide for the full editing steps.

## Rules that apply throughout

1. **Never reveal or hint at the planted error.** Do not mention dates, mistakes, errors, checking or correcting. Do not direct the participant's attention towards the drafts, and do not inspect them in a way that signals something is wrong.
2. **Never point at, name, read out or describe any text, label or button.** This includes the labels in Version B. Do not ask "did you see the labels?" or "did you notice anything?".
3. **Do not lead.** Ask what they expect or think. Do not ask yes or no questions that suggest an answer.
4. **Do not suggest sending or not sending, or editing or not editing.** Only the participant decides.
5. **Do not explain any product rule or word.** If asked what something means, say "What do you expect it to mean?"
6. **Do not say whether any answer is right or wrong.** This includes the knowledge check. Do not react with praise, surprise or correction.
7. **Do not explain anything between versions.** Say only what this guide and the general test plan tell you to say.
8. **Keep your face and tone neutral.** Do not nod at answers.

## What to say when

| Moment | You say |
|---|---|
| Start | The introduction in the general test plan, with the extra sentence above |
| Before the first version | The scenario in the general test plan, then stop talking |
| The participant goes quiet | "What are you thinking?" |
| The participant has just pressed or is about to press something | "What do you expect to happen?" |
| The participant asks "Is this right?" | "What would you do if this were real?" |
| The participant asks what a button or word means | "What do you expect it to do?" or "What do you expect it to mean?" |
| The participant opens an edit screen | "Please tell me out loud what you would change and I will show you the result." |
| The participant finishes or stops the first version | "Thank you. Please fill in Section 1 of this form." Hand over the response template |
| Between versions | Nothing about the prototype. Press **R** in the other version and say only the second-version instruction |
| The participant finishes or stops the second version | "Thank you. Please fill in Section 2 of this form." |
| After Section 2 | Ask the four comparison questions aloud (Section 3) |

## Observed, tested and stated: keep them apart

- **Observed:** what you see and hear during the task, written on the observation sheet. Think-aloud remarks count as observed behaviour.
- **Tested:** the knowledge check. Do not look at the answers during the session and do not score them yet.
- **Stated:** ratings and the answers to open questions.

Do not mix them. Do not write your own interpretation as if it were something the participant said. Write exact words first and interpretation separately.

## The second exposure

- The participant now knows the screens and the planted content. Treat the second run as affected by learning and by the questions asked after the first version. It is supplementary and not an independent first impression.
- Do not count noticing, correcting or the reasons for sending on the second run. Fill in the observation sheet, but skip Section 3.
- If the participant notices or mentions the planted error during the second run, write their exact words in "Other notes" on the second-exposure observation sheet. Do not comment, react or ask about it.

## If something goes wrong

| Situation | You do |
|---|---|
| A prototype breaks or a timer misfires | Note the screen and what happened. Press **R** and say "Let's pick it up from the start of that part." |
| The participant realises there are two prototypes | Do not explain. Say "Please go ahead as you would normally." |
| The participant asks which version is the better or newer one | "I would like to hear what you think after both." |
| The participant compares versions during the task | Write it down. Do not respond. |
| The participant asks you to confirm an answer on the form | "There are no right or wrong answers. Please answer as you see it." |
| The participant presses a key by accident | Press **R** if the state is wrong and note it. |

## After each session

1. Press **R** in both prototypes.
2. Fill in both observation sheets within an hour.
3. Put the participant's response template away unscored. Score it after all six sessions.
4. Note any point where your words or actions may have helped the participant, in the conduct section of each observation sheet.

## After the sixth session

Score the knowledge checks with the answer key in [comparison-and-findings-template.md](comparison-and-findings-template.md). Do not show the key to participants, ever.
